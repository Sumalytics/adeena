# Two-Set Problems Using Venn Diagrams
**Authorized MVP specimen:** newly authored demonstration text; arithmetic checks must be deterministic.

A set is a collection of distinct elements. A two-set Venn diagram uses two overlapping circles to show membership in two sets A and B. The overlapping region, called the intersection, contains elements that belong to both sets. The union contains elements in A or B or both.

To count the union without double-counting the overlap: |A ∪ B| = |A| + |B| - |A ∩ B|.

Example: In a class of 30 learners, 18 play football, 14 play basketball, and 6 play both. The intersection contains 6. Football only = 18 - 6 = 12. Basketball only = 14 - 6 = 8. In at least one set = 12 + 6 + 8 = 26. Neither = 30 - 26 = 4.

## Visual explanation goals
Animate two labeled circles appearing and overlapping. Place 6 counters into the intersection first, then 12 into football-only, 8 into basketball-only, and 4 outside both circles. Show why adding 18 and 14 directly double-counts the intersection.

## Practical learner checks
Drag or select number groups into the correct Venn regions (keyboard/touch alternatives required). Solve union and neither using the inclusion-exclusion formula. Show visual, mathematically accurate corrective feedback for incorrect placement or arithmetic.
