# How the Human Respiratory System Works
**Authorized MVP specimen:** newly authored demonstration text. Science accuracy must be independently reviewed before publishing to children; this file is not medical advice.

Breathing moves air into and out of the lungs. When a person inhales, the diaphragm contracts and moves downward while the chest cavity expands, helping air enter the lungs. When a person exhales normally, the diaphragm relaxes upward as air moves out.

Air travels from the nose or mouth through the trachea into two bronchi, which branch into smaller bronchioles inside the lungs. These end near tiny air sacs called alveoli. Oxygen diffuses from the alveoli into blood in nearby capillaries. Carbon dioxide diffuses from the blood into the alveoli and is exhaled.

## Visual explanation goals
Show the path of air clearly. Distinguish airflow through airways from gas exchange across the alveolar-capillary barrier. Animate the diaphragm and chest changes, then magnify an alveolus. Do not draw blood travelling through the bronchi or oxygen becoming carbon dioxide.

## Practical learner checks
Sequence the airway path. Label the trachea, bronchi, lungs and alveoli. Identify the direction of oxygen and carbon dioxide exchange. Show corrective visual feedback for confusing bronchi with alveoli.
